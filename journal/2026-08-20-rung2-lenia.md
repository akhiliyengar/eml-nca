# Rung 2: Erez's gate fails in Lenia — and the reason is his own centering term

thread: `t2-lenia-gate`
status: **hypothesis falsified as published**, with a mechanism and a working repair
run: `results/t2-lenia-gate/57182679d86c.metrics.json`

## Expected

This was the highest-prior hypothesis in the spec. Lenia's growth function is
univariate and non-monotone — exactly the shape Erez's gate produces natively,
and exactly *not* the cross-variable shape that sank Rung 1. I expected it to
work.

## Observed

Matched design: both arms run the same 20-seed grid (5 radii × 4 amplitudes).

| arm | configurations | best basin | total |
|---|---:|---:|---:|
| Gaussian (control) | 9 | **19/20** | 71/180 |
| Erez, as published | 120 | **0/20** | **0/2400** |
| Erez − constant (repair) | 12 | **20/20** | 52/240 |

**Falsified.** Zero survivors across 2400 runs while the control survived on
the same seeds — so this is about the growth function, not the seeds.

### The mechanism

Growth value at low neighbour activity, which is where it matters:

| | u=0.001 | u=0.01 | u=0.05 |
|---|---:|---:|---:|
| Gaussian | **−1.0** | **−1.0** | **−0.999** |
| Erez | **+0.0036** | +0.030 | +0.088 |

**The Erez gate is non-negative across the entire positive domain.** It rises
and falls — the peak is real, `R* = (a/b)^(1/(1−a))` checks out — but the
falling part never crosses zero. So empty space cannot decay. Any trace of
activity grows, and the grid fills with fog.

The cause is Erez's own **centering term `−c^a`**, the piece that pins
`G(0) = 0`. In his ODE setting that is right: a quiescent state should stay
quiescent. In a cellular automaton it is fatal, because a CA needs sparse
regions to *actively die*, not merely hold still.

### The repair

Predicted before testing: subtract a constant, restore decay, get creatures.

Confirmed — `G(x) = (c+x)^a − b·x − c^a − θ` at `a=0.25, b=1.0, θ=0.1` gives
**20/20**, marginally beating the control's 19/20.

## Interpretation

**This nearly went the other way, for the second rung running.**

The first sweep reported **20/20 survivors** for the published gate and printed
*"HYPOTHESIS SUPPORTED"*. It was false. My judge only weighed the grid, and a
screen of uniform dim grey weighs the same as a bright creature on black. The
measured separation once I looked:

| | grid left empty |
|---|---|
| real creatures | 81%, 99.6% |
| fog | 0.0%, 5.7% |

Nothing in between. Adding "at least half the world must stay empty" turned
20/20 into 0/20.

**Two rungs, two false positives, both from a criterion measuring the wrong
thing.** Rung 1 measured error on the fitted domain when it should have
measured extrapolation. Rung 2 measured mass when it should have measured
structure. The pattern is worth naming: *the failure mode of a research harness
is not a wrong answer, it is a right answer to a question you did not mean to
ask.*

**On the parsimony claim.** The repair costs a fourth parameter — `a, b, c, θ`
against the Gaussian's `mu, sigma`. And Erez's "3 knobs instead of 6" was
measured against **Hill functions**, which are monotone and genuinely need two
opposed blocks for a rise-then-fall. Lenia's bump is *already* non-monotone with
two parameters. **The comparison Erez made does not transfer to this target** —
his baseline was worse than Lenia's, so beating his baseline says nothing here.

**What this does NOT mean.** Not "EML is useless". It shows one specific design
choice, correct in its original context, is wrong in this one — and a
one-parameter change fixes it. That is a more useful result than either a clean
pass or a flat failure.

Also worth stating plainly: the repaired gate *works*, at 20/20 vs the control's
19/20. If the parameter count were not a regression, this would be a positive
result. It is the accounting that sinks it, not the dynamics.

## Next

Two candidates, in order:

1. **`t2b-erez-offset`** — a new thread for the repaired gate. Is 20/20 robust
   across kernel radius and dt, or only at these settings? Does the θ term have
   an interpretation, or is it a fudge factor? A fudge factor that works is
   still worth reporting, but it should be labelled as one.

2. **Rung 3** — the actual NCA target. Rung 2's lesson transfers directly: an
   NCA update rule also needs cells to be able to *decrease*, and any EML-based
   rule inherits the same non-negativity problem unless offset.

Carry forward as defaults, now three items:
- extrapolation split on every fit *(Rung 1)*
- control ratio, never absolute error *(Rung 1)*
- **structure check, never mass alone** *(Rung 2)*
