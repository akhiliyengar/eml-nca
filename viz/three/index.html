growth function
Why two buttons? The left one is the rule Lenia already uses. You need it to compare against — “the new rule kept it alive” means nothing on its own. Alive compared to what?
Gaussian G(u)
In plain English: a cell is happy only with just the right amount of neighbour activity. Like porridge temperature — too little or too much and it dims. mu is the right amount, sigma is how fussy it is.
Erez gate (c+x)^a − b·x − c^a
In plain English: two forces pulling against each other. a grows with neighbours but gets tired as it rises. b is a steady penalty that never tires. Early on growth wins; later the penalty overtakes it. So the curve goes up, turns over, comes down. c pins the curve so empty space stays empty.

kernel & time
radius = how far a cell looks. It looks at a ring of neighbours, not a blob — caring about distance is what makes shapes crawl instead of sit still.
dt = size of one time step. Too big and the simulation jumps past reality, like a flipbook missing pages.

verdict—
Gain = how much bigger it got in one step. Sounds boring; it is not. The rule runs thousands of times and small things multiply: 1.05 per step becomes 131.5× after 100 steps, 0.95 becomes 0.0059. Picture a microphone next to its own speaker — ear-splitting squeal or dead silence, almost nothing in between.

verdict also checks the world is not fog. A screen of dim grey weighs the same as a bright creature on black, so we require most of the grid stays empty. That check changed a result: a run scoring 20/20 turned out to be fog, and the true score was 0.
seed pattern
Where the creature starts. The set of starting shapes that survive is tiny — radius 8 at amplitude 0.50 dies by step 22, at 0.60 it lives past 400, and radius 10 dies for everything tried. That is why the experiment sweeps many seeds: otherwise “the new rule killed it” is indistinguishable from “that seed was doomed anyway”.
